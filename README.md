# Intergenerational Mobility and Nonlinear Human Capital Returns

This repository contains all materials for a research project on **intergenerational mobility**, with a central focus on **non-linear returns to human capital** and their implications for persistence and inequality across generations.

The project develops a theoretical framework in which **market returns to human capital are increasing and feature discrete thresholds**, generating endogenous sorting of families into distinct investment regimes.

---

## Project Overview

We study how **non-linear returns to human capital** shape intergenerational mobility.  
In the model, parents optimally invest in their children’s human capital facing increasing and threshold-based market returns. This structure implies a **highly non-linear mapping from parental income to investment decisions**: small differences in parental income can lead to large, discrete changes in optimal investment.

As a result:

- Families endogenously sort into **low- and high-investment regimes**
- Inequality becomes **persistent**, even in the absence of ex-ante heterogeneity
- Intergenerational mobility is reduced, particularly **at the top of the income distribution**

The framework provides a mechanism-consistent interpretation of empirical patterns commonly associated with the *Great Gatsby Curve*, without relying on linear transmission processes.

## Status

- Project stage: **theoretical development / early draft**
- Core model: **intergenerational investment with non-convex returns**
- Extensions under consideration:  
  - Progressive taxation with distint marginal tax rate. 
  - How changes in the rate of return of human capital investments affect inequality and mobility.
